## Why Rest Matters in Open Source

Open source maintainership is sustainable only when maintainers practice self-care. This includes taking regular breaks from the project.

**This policy establishes:**
- Normal and expected break patterns
- How to communicate breaks to the community
- How the project continues during breaks
- How to return from breaks

**This policy applies to:**
- All current and future maintainers
- All types of maintenance work (issues, PRs, releases, etc.)
- Both planned and unplanned breaks

---

## Normal Break Patterns

### Weekly Breaks

**Standard:** Maintainers are expected to take at least one full day off per week from Surypus work.

**Recommended:** Take weekends off, or choose one weekday as your break day.

**Communication:** You don't need to announce weekly breaks. Just don't work on your break day.

### Monthly Breaks

**Standard:** Maintainers are encouraged to take one longer break per month (3-7 days).

**Recommended:** Schedule a quarterly long weekend or week off.

**Communication:** Post in a GitHub issue or discussion 1-2 weeks before a longer break.

**Template:**
```
I'm planning to take a break from Surypus maintenance from [date] to [date].
I won't be reviewing PRs or responding to issues during this time.
[Backup maintainer] can help with urgent matters.
I'll be back on [return date].
```

### Quarterly Breaks

**Standard:** Maintainers are encouraged to take at least one week off per quarter (4 weeks per year).

**Recommended:** Schedule one week of reduced activity or full break each quarter.

**Communication:** Post 2-4 weeks before the break.

### Annual Breaks

**Standard:** Maintainers are expected to take at least 2 weeks off per year.

**Recommended:** Take a full vacation or sabbatical each year.

**Communication:** Post 4-6 weeks before a long break.

## Planned Breaks

### How to Request a Planned Break

1. **Decide:** Determine the dates and scope of your break
2. **Communicate:** Post in the maintainer discussion or issue
3. **Delegate:** Identify tasks that need coverage and who can handle them
4. **Update Status:** Set your GitHub status to "On vacation" or similar
5. **Go:** Take your break without guilt!

### Break Request Template

```
## Planned Break Request

**Maintainer:** [your name]
**Dates:** [start date] to [end date]
**Scope:** [full break / reduced activity / no reviews / etc.]

**Coverage Plan:**
- [ ] Issues: [who will triage?]
- [ ] PRs: [who will review?]
- [ ] Releases: [who will handle?]
- [ ] Other: [any other needs]

**Notes:**
[Any additional context]
```

### During Your Break

- Actually step away from the project
- Don't feel guilty about not working
- Trust your coverage plan
- Check in only if you want to

### Returning from a Break

1. **Catch up gradually:** Start with small tasks
2. **Reconnect:** Read recent discussions and catch up on context
3. **Reflect:** Did the break help? What would you change next time?
4. **Resume normal work:** Return to your regular maintenance rhythm

## Unplanned Breaks

Sometimes you need a break unexpectedly—due to illness, personal emergencies, burnout, or simply needing space.

### Short Unplanned Breaks (1-3 days)

- No need to announce unless it's an emergency
- Set your GitHub status if you're not responding
- Resume when you're able

### Medium Unplanned Breaks (1-2 weeks)

- Post briefly if you're able: "I need to step away for a bit. I'll be back around [date]."
- If you have backup maintainers, let them know
- Set your GitHub status

### Long Unplanned Breaks (2+ weeks)

- Post what you can: "I need to take an extended break. I'll be back when I can. Thank you for understanding."
- If possible, ask a backup maintainer to cover urgent matters
- Set your GitHub status

### Mental Health Days

Taking a day off for mental health is valid and encouraged. You don't need to explain or justify it.

- Take the day
- Set your status if you want
- Return when you're ready

## Burnout Prevention

### Signs You Need a Break

- Feeling drained or exhausted by the project
- Dreading opening issues or PRs
- Feeling resentful toward contributors or users
- Having trouble focusing or making decisions
- Physical symptoms (headaches, sleep problems, etc.)
- Withdrawing from the community

### What to Do

1. **Acknowledge:** Recognize that you're burning out
2. **Take a break:** Step away from the project
3. **Seek support:** Talk to other maintainers or a professional
4. **Consider changes:** What would make this sustainable long-term?

### Returning After Burnout

- Be patient with yourself
- Start small
- Consider what changes would help prevent burnout in the future
- It's okay to step back further if needed

## Encouraging a Healthy Culture

This policy exists to normalize rest and self-care in the Surypus community.

**We expect:**
- Maintainers take breaks
- Maintainers support each other's breaks
- Contributors understand and respect break policies
- No guilt trips or pressure to work during breaks

**We discourage:**
- Guilt-tripping maintainers for taking breaks
- Expecting 24/7 availability
- Punishing maintainers for taking time off
- Creating urgency that prevents breaks
